Nearly every effort to improve the world eventually depends on someone learning something well.

A health worker putting new evidence into practice. A public servant applying a law that just changed. A community claiming a right it already holds.

How well that learning is designed shapes how much of the intended effect reaches practice.

Learning design is the practice that makes that difference deliberate.

It rests on a shared craft — diagnosis, scoping, working with experts, and plain language, held to a common standard of evidence appraisal, accessibility, and ethics.

This practice has grown across ten branches of expertise:

  • Learning Experience Design
  • Public Learning
  • Universal Design for Learning
  • Knowledge Mobilization
  • Evaluation
  • Learning Systems Design
  • Human Rights Education
  • Health Workforce Education
  • Accessibility
  • Epistemology

Not ten separate skills — one practice, seen from ten perspectives.
