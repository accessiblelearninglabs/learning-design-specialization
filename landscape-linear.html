Learning Design Specialization

A learning design practice, mapped

Much of what people need in order to act — on their health, their rights, their work — is not missing but unusable: built without them, for conditions that are not theirs. Learning design is where those choices are made. This practice develops knowledge through interaction with the people it concerns, and treats access as a condition of the work rather than a correction to it. This page sets out part of that practice: a trunk of shared craft, and nine branches of expertise.

Section 1

How this map is organized

A single practice that designs how people and organizations learn, expressed as a trunk of shared craft and nine connected branches of expertise — each with its own node and its own page. The trunk is what everything else grows from: the diagnostic question asked before anything is built, the writing, the visual language, the review against shared standards, the ethical and legal obligations the work carries, and the practices that keep it checkable and current. Every contribution below grows on one branch and links to the others it draws on, because the branches are not separate skills but one body of work seen from different perspectives.

The interactive version arranges the same material two ways: by branch, which shows where each contribution grows, and by affinity, which repositions contributions by how alike they are, so relationships across branches become visible.

A small number of contributions grow on two branches at once. Each appears once here, under its home branch, and states its second membership. A branch page may therefore list more resources than this page groups under that branch.

The shared craft of learning design, which every branch grows from and none of them owns. It runs from the first diagnostic question — whether this is a learning problem at all — through scoping, working with experts, writing, visual language and production, and out to the practices the field holds in common: quality assurance, source discipline, privacy, ethics, update cycles, and the case made for the resource in the first place.

Epistemology

The conditions under which a knowledge claim is admitted, discounted, or never formed at all — the questions that arise before evidence is appraised rather than during it. This branch gathers the contributions that give a design process language for how it decides what will count as knowledge, and for what the exclusions cost. Evaluation

The design of the evidence a learning programme produces about itself. This branch gathers the contributions that build measurement into practice from the first cycle rather than adding it at the end: what can be established when there is no baseline, what can be claimed at small numbers, how a portfolio is measured when capacity covers only part of it, and how findings are shaped so that a decision can rest on them. Telling a Learning Need from a Performance Problem

The question asked before anything is designed

Planned

A request for training usually arrives attached to a problem training cannot fix — unclear expectations, missing tools, broken incentives, understaffing, a marking load nobody costed, a prerequisite that quietly stopped being taught. Diagnosing which is which, before any design begins, changes the outcome more than any later decision will. It is also the step most often skipped.

Minimum Viable Content

Deciding what to cut, before anything is built

Planned

The instinct to cover everything is the most reliable source of bloated, unfinishable learning. What to Build When the Answer Isn’t a Course

Support placed at the moment the work happens

Planned

Job aids, checklists, decision aids at the point of care, protocol cards, a worked-example set, a rubric released before the work starts, a clearer brief, a page that answers the question forty people are about to ask. Plain Language and Writing for Learning

Reading load as a design decision

Planned

How hard something is to read is usually the biggest barrier to understanding it, and it has nothing to do with the subject itself. It is also the barrier designers most often leave to chance. Reading load decides whether a patient follows their discharge instructions, whether a public notice meets its plain-language duty, whether a researcher can actually use a method someone has described, and whether a student can tell what an assignment is asking them to do.

How Learning Works, and Who Can Act on It

Told plainly to learners, and built into the design

Planned

Most learners have never been told how consolidation works, why re-reading feels productive and isn’t, or that difficulty is often the signal rather than the failure. This makes that knowledge available in plain language as something offered rather than assigned, and builds the regulating into the design — spacing in the schedule, retrieval in the activity, requirements stated plainly — because every strategy in this literature presupposes conditions that unequal circumstances do not distribute equally.

Writing Outcomes That Can Be Assessed

Holding outcome, evidence and activity in one line

Planned

Assessment When the Answer Can Be Generated

Designing tasks a model cannot sit in for

Planned

Detection is unreliable, adversarial, and falls hardest on the people least able to contest it. The durable answers are in the task itself: work anchored to a particular context, a process that leaves a trail, evidence gathered in person at the points where it genuinely matters, and stated expectations about permitted use rather than a blanket prohibition nobody can enforce. The prior question is which capability a task is protecting, and whether it is worth protecting there.

Grounding. Assessment security (Dawson); permitted-use scales (Perkins et al.); construct validity (Messick)

When the Consultation Is the Whole Intervention

One conversation, and no project behind it

Planned

A great deal of this work is not a project. It is an hour with someone who has a course, a deadline, and the option of simply not writing back. What can be diagnosed, decided, and left behind in a single conversation is a different craft from running a scoped build, and it is where most of a practice’s actual reach lives. Designing that hour — what to ask, what to skip, what to leave in their hands — deserves the same attention as designing anything else.

The Right to Education Itself

Planned
